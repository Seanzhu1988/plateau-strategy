How backing was built

Idea, to blueprint, to payment. The path a person walks, and the rails that keep it safe.

The path a backer walks

1An idea sits on the board

Anyone can pitch one, free, no account. It shows what they want to build and which trades it will take.

↓
2They tap "Back this project"

The old "register to invest" and "register to launch" forms are gone. One button now: back it by getting the blueprint.

↓
3The Blueprint Deck opens

The lab's blueprints, each with a short teaser anyone can read and a price the author sets.

↓
4They log in and pay

Sign in with Google, which fills a name and email, then pay the price. The card is charged through Square.

↓
5The payment settles, access is granted

Only a settled payment adds a reader to the unlock list. Nobody reaches a blueprint by asking.

↓
6They read the full blueprint

The sheet is served only to a reader on the unlock list. That is the whole of it: backing a project is buying its blueprint.

The rails that keep it safe

  • A purchase, not an investment You get the document, no stake and no share of profit. The "payment becomes a stake" idea is a securities matter and is deliberately left out, waiting for the lawyer.
  • The paywall is real The blueprint file is sent only to a buyer, never to the page, so it cannot be reached with the view-source menu.
  • No money moves by accident Card payment is off until Square is connected. Until then the deck still works, and the owner grants access by hand.
  • The author holds the price You set it and change it any time. Paying never gives a buyer a claim on your work.
  • Phone first Every screen here is designed narrow first and checked on a phone before it is called done.

Two switches turn it fully on

Google sign-in appears once GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID is set. The button already ships and hides itself until then.
Card payment works once Square is connected (SQUARE_ACCESS_TOKEN and SQUARE_LOCATION_ID). Both are yours to set, and neither exposes a key in the code.

Plateau Strategy Solution Lab. Built phone first, a purchase not an investment, and nothing switched on until you say so.
