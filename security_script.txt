When you book a ride here, you give us your name. Most companies write that
down and keep it. We do something else with it.

Your name is not one secret. It is two.

The moment you book, your name is split in half. Your first name goes into one
vault. Your family name goes into another. Each vault has its own key, and the
two keys are never kept in the same place.

Here is why that matters. If somebody broke in and stole one of those vaults,
they would be holding a list of surnames. Chen. Alvarez. Okafor. A surname on
its own belongs to millions of people and points at none of them. It is only
when a family name is joined to a first name, a phone number, and the address
where you will not be home on Tuesday, that it becomes you.

Your driver is told your first name, so they know who they are meeting. That is
one vault. The other half stays shut.

To see your whole name, the owner has to sign in and write down why. That is
not a formality. It is recorded, an alert goes out, and each record is locked to
the one before it, so a record cannot be quietly changed or removed later
without the break showing.

We cannot promise nobody will ever try. We can promise that if they do, what
they get is half a name, and that every time somebody looks, it leaves a mark.
