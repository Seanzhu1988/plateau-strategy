This is a walk through the middle of Yale, ten stops, under a mile, and it starts on a public square that is older than the university and ends in a concert hall with the names of the dead cut into the walls.

We go in through the arch from the New Haven Green, into the quadrangle where every first year student lives, and stop at the only building left of the colonial college, from seventeen fifty two, with Nathan Hale standing outside it with his hands tied. Then the tower, two hundred and sixteen feet of stone that was deliberately aged with acid and hand chipped edges the year it was built, with fifty four bells at the top that students play twice a day. Then the two art museums facing each other across Chapel Street, the first building Louis Kahn ever made his name with and the last one he made before he died, both free, one with Van Gogh's Night Cafe and one with the largest collection of British art outside Britain. Then the lawn with Maya Lin's fountain on it, a spiral of numbers counting the women enrolled at Yale each year, which begins with a very long run of zeros. Then the library built as a cathedral, with the circulation desk where the altar would be. Then the marble box with no windows, whose walls are cut thin enough to glow, holding a Gutenberg Bible and a manuscript nobody has ever read. And last, the hall with the great organ and the war memorial rotunda.

Nothing on this walk is ticketed and both museums are free. Come on a weekday if you want the carillon; they play about half past twelve. Look at the stone everywhere and remember that most of it is younger than the aeroplane. Let us start on the Green.
