This is a walk round Brown, eleven stops on top of College Hill above Providence, about a mile, on the smallest and least formal of the eight campuses on this tour and in some ways the most interesting.

We start at a pair of iron gates that open twice a year, inward in September for the arriving class and outward in May for the leaving one, and which students will not walk through at any other time. Then the brick hall of seventeen seventy that was the entire college and was a barracks and a hospital in the Revolution, with French troops quartered in it on the march to Yorktown. Then a Greek temple copied from a specific building at Eleusis and doubled in size. Then the hall with every president of the university painted on its walls, where we talk about the report Brown published in two thousand and six on its own founding money and the slave trade. Then a library of the first books ever printed about the Americas. Then a tower a widowed Italian built for his wife, with love is strong as death cut into the base. Then the library of Lincoln's secretary, holding Lovecraft's papers, half a million pictures of soldiers and three books bound in human skin. Then the student centre, where we explain the curriculum with no requirements in it. Then the concrete tower everybody was taught to dislike. Then a war memorial you walk under rather than visit. And last, a street of two hundred colonial houses that survived because the city got poor at the right moment.

Nothing here is ticketed. Let us start at the gates, and do not walk through the middle one.
