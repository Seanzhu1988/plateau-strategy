This is a walk through the city where the country was written, and it goes from the room where it happened to the hill where the city looks back at itself. Nineteen stops. The first thirteen are the old city, within a few streets of each other: the Liberty Bell, Independence Hall and the two smaller halls where Congress and the Supreme Court first sat, the Greek temple of the Second Bank with the faces of the founders inside, the guild hall where the first Congress met, the museum with Washington's tent, Franklin's ghost house and his post office, the church with the tallest steeple in the colonies, the house of the woman who is said to have sewn the flag, the oldest street of houses in the country, and the museum of the Constitution looking back at the Hall. Then the walk crosses the modern city: the market under the railway shed, the largest city hall in the country with William Penn on top of it, the letters in the plaza, the Rodin, the museum on the hill with the seventy two steps, and the prison that invented solitary confinement.

It is about four miles in all, and nobody has to do all of it. The old city is a morning. The Parkway is an afternoon, and the bus runs up it. Get your ticket for Independence Hall first, at the Visitor Center, because they run out, and let its time shape the day. I will be beside you at every stop, telling you the one thing to look at and the one thing to do. Let us start at Sixth and Market.
