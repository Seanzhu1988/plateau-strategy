This is a walk through Princeton, eleven stops, about a mile, and it takes you through four different centuries of people building what they thought a university ought to look like, each convinced the last lot had got it wrong.

We start on the town square, cross one street, and go in through a gate that stands permanently open because a graduating class asked for it, and that students still refuse to walk out of until the day they graduate. Then the building that was the whole college in seventeen fifty six, took cannon fire in the Revolution, and was the capitol of the United States for four months in seventeen eighty three. Then the lawn behind it with a Revolutionary cannon buried muzzle down in the middle to stop Rutgers stealing it again. Then a Venetian octagon that was the library and is now a cafe under a stained glass dome. From there the route goes to Firestone Library, most of which is underground; one of the largest university chapels in the world, finished just as compulsory chapel was ending; the villa where Woodrow Wilson lived and where his wife laid out the garden that inspired her White House rose garden; the art museum whose new building opened in twenty twenty five; the great arch that used to lead down to a railway station; and last, the Romanesque hall that everybody hated for a century and everybody now admires.

Nothing here is ticketed. The campus is open and the museum is free. Come in term if you can, when the buildings are open and there are people on the lawns, and check the museum's official page for that day's hours. Let us start on the square.
