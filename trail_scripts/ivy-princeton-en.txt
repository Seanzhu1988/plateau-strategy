This is a walk through Princeton, eleven stops, about a mile, and it takes you through four different centuries of people building what they thought a university ought to look like, each convinced the last lot had got it wrong.

We start on the town square, cross one street, and go in through a gate that stands permanently open because a graduating class asked for it, and that students still refuse to walk out of until the day they graduate. Then the building that was the whole college in seventeen fifty six, took cannon fire in the Revolution, and was the capitol of the United States for four months in seventeen eighty three. Then the lawn behind it with a Revolutionary cannon buried muzzle down in the middle to stop Rutgers stealing it again. Then a Venetian octagon that was the library and is now a cafe under a stained glass dome. Then one of the largest university chapels in the world, finished just as compulsory chapel was ending. Then the library that is mostly underground, the art museum that has just been rebuilt, the villa where Woodrow Wilson lived and where his wife laid out the garden that became the White House rose garden, the great arch that used to lead down to a railway station, and last, the Romanesque hall that everybody hated for a century and everybody now admires.

Nothing here is ticketed. The campus is open and the museum is free. Come in term if you can, when the buildings are open and there are people on the lawns. Let us start on the square.
