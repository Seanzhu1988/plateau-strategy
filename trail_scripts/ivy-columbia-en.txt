This is a walk across Columbia, ten stops in about half a mile, on the most complete Beaux Arts campus in America: one domed library at the top of a flight of steps, thirty quiet brick buildings arranged around it, and six blocks of Manhattan holding a university of thirty six thousand people.

We start at the gates on Broadway and walk in along the axis the whole place was built on. Then the seated bronze woman on the steps with an owl hidden in her gown that every student is told to find. Then the domed library that has not been a library since nineteen thirty four and is now where the university's arguments arrive. Then the working library across the green with the names of the great writers cut into the stone, all of them men, all of them ancient, which students have been arguing about since the nineteen eighties. Then the empty stone drum in the middle of the campus that lost its sixteen ton granite sphere in nineteen forty six and became the place people stand to make speeches. Then the college building that has been occupied five times in sixty years, the philosophy building with Rodin's Thinker outside it and the invention of FM radio in its basement, the chapel with the Catalan tile vaulting and the best acoustics on the campus, the little domed hall that was built for religion in nineteen oh two and now houses a dozen faiths and none, and last, the physics building where the uranium atom was split on American soil for the first time.

The campus has controlled access at times, so check before you come. Everything outdoors is free. Let us start at the gates.
