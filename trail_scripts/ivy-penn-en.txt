This is a walk along Locust Walk, the brick spine of the University of Pennsylvania, ten stops in about a mile, on the campus of the one Ivy League school that was founded by a man who never went to university himself.

We start on a bench with Benjamin Franklin sitting on it reading his own newspaper, because he founded this place and he left room for you. Then the Walk itself, which was a public street with cars on it until the nineteen sixties and became a campus when the city let Penn close it. Then a sixteen foot aluminium button, split down the middle, that popped off Franklin's waistcoat and rolled down the lawn. Then the green stone building that has been crumbling since eighteen seventy three because the fashionable stone of that decade cannot survive frost. Then the finest Victorian library in America, by an architect his own century came to despise and two thirds of whose buildings were pulled down. Then the first student union ever built, anywhere. Then a concert hall with one of the world's largest organs in it, designed in an office by a man whose name was kept off the drawings because he was Black. Then the museum with a twelve and a half ton sphinx in the entrance hall and the treasure of Ur upstairs. And last, the oldest football stadium in the country, where the crowd throws toast.

The campus is open, the Walk is public, and the museum is the one paid stop. Franklin's bench is at Thirty Seventh Street. Let us start there.
