This is a walk round Dartmouth, eleven stops, about half a mile, and the whole of it fits in a square you could cross in five minutes. It is the smallest of the eight on this tour, in the smallest town, an hour and a half from a city, on the edge of the woods, and everything about it follows from that.

We start on the porch of the college's own inn, looking across a five acre green that has been open since the seventeen seventies and where they still build a bonfire every autumn and a snow sculpture every winter. Then the row of white clapboard buildings that generated one of the foundational cases in American constitutional law, argued by an alumnus who told the Supreme Court it was a small college and yet there were those who loved it. Then a Romanesque chapel from eighteen eighty five. Then the rare book library that lets undergraduates handle medieval manuscripts without an appointment, and which holds Dr Seuss's papers and one of the great polar collections in the world. Then the library with twenty four fresco panels by José Clemente Orozco in the basement reading room, one of the major works of twentieth century art, with students revising underneath it. Then the panelled room where tea has been served at four o'clock on weekdays since nineteen twenty eight. Then Parkhurst Hall, the administration building occupied by antiwar demonstrators in nineteen sixty nine. From there we continue to the student centre, the arts centre with the post office deliberately built inside it, and last a museum holding Assyrian palace reliefs from eight hundred years before Christ.

Nothing is ticketed and the museum is free. Come at four if you can, for the tea. Let us start on the porch.
