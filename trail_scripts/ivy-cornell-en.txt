This is a walk round Cornell, eleven stops, about a mile with one real climb, on a hill four hundred feet above a lake with a gorge on either side and waterfalls inside the campus.

Cornell is the youngest of the eight by a century, the largest, and the least tidy, and it was founded in eighteen sixty five by a carpenter who had laid the first telegraph line and a young academic with ideas, on the principle that any person could find instruction in any study. That sentence is on everything here, and in eighteen sixty five every word of it was radical.

We start at the crossroads by the student union, then climb to the clock tower, where students play twenty one bells three times a day and you may climb a hundred and sixty one steps and stand beside them while they do it. Then the library with the great iron and wood reading room, and the lawsuit that cost the university a fortune. Then the slope with the best view on any campus in this country, forty miles down a lake. Then the old quadrangle with the two founders in bronze facing each other across the grass. Then the humanities building with the cafe named after a room of plaster gods nobody kept. Then the chapel where both founders are buried. Then the president's own house. Then I. M. Pei's concrete museum on the lip of a gorge, with an Asian gallery whose end wall is a window over the lake. Then a suspension bridge a hundred and forty feet above a creek, and last the gorge itself.

Everything here is free. Come for a chimes concert if you can. Let us start on the plaza.
