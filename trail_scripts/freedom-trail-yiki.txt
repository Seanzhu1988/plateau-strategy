This is the Freedom Trail, and the first thing to know is that it is a line you
can see. A strip of red brick set into the pavement, running two and a half
miles from Boston Common up to the Bunker Hill Monument. You do not need a map.
You follow the brick.

Sixteen sites sit along it. The walking between them is only about forty five
minutes altogether, which surprises people. What takes the day is stopping. If
you go inside all sixteen properly you are looking at six hours, so most people
walk the first eleven, which are all downtown and take about three and a half.
The last five cross the river into Charlestown, and that is a decision worth
making before you start rather than at four in the afternoon.

A few things that will make the day better.

Wear the shoes you would wear to walk all morning. The trail is cobblestone in
places and the old streets are uneven.

Go north, from the Common toward Charlestown. Everyone does, so the sites open
in the order the story happened, and the afternoon sun stays behind you.

You can join it anywhere and leave it anywhere. Nobody is checking. If you have
two hours, walk from the Common to Faneuil Hall and stop there. That is the
part where the argument for revolution was actually made, and it is the densest
mile of the whole thing.

And this is a working city, not a museum. The Old Corner Bookstore is a burrito
shop. People live above the sites. The graveyards are wedged between office
buildings, and you will hear traffic the whole way. That is not a flaw in the
trail. It is what makes it different from a reconstruction: these things stayed
where they happened while the city grew around them.

Start at the Common, find the brick, and turn right.
