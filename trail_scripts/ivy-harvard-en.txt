This is a walk round Harvard Yard, which is the oldest college ground in the country and is smaller than you expect. Ten stops, about a mile, and you could cross the whole of it in ten minutes if you did not stop, which is the point: this is a walk for looking closely rather than covering ground.

We start on the town side, in Harvard Square, with the subway and the newsstand and the traffic, and go in through the big gate with the founding vote carved on one pier and, on the other, the sentence about dreading to leave an illiterate ministry. Then the oldest building Harvard has, from seventeen twenty, with the president of the university on the ground floor and first year students asleep on the top. Then the statue everybody photographs, where all three statements in the inscription are false. Then the church built to the dead of the First World War, with the names cut into the wall, and across the lawn from it the library a mother built after her son went down with the Titanic. Then a brick building by the best American architect of the nineteenth century, with an arch that carries a whisper twelve feet. Then the art museums, free, with the conservation laboratories behind glass and a wall of pigment in jars. Then the great red Victorian pile that is a war memorial, a concert hall and a dining room at once. And last, the concrete building at the top of the plaza, from where you can see the whole of what you have just walked.

Come in the morning if you can, before the tour groups, when the Yard is quiet enough for the whispering arch to work. Nothing here is ticketed and the art museums cost nothing. Do not try the doors of the dormitories; people live behind them. Let us start on the town side of the wall.
