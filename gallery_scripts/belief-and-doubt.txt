Belief+Doubt. Barbara Kruger, 2012. The lower level, the lobby at the bottom of
the escalator, running on into the bookstore. No ticket, the Hirshhorn is free.

Start with the reason you have probably already missed it. The escalator down is
the way to the bathrooms and the shop, so people ride it looking at a phone,
cross the largest artwork in this building, buy a postcard of something upstairs
and ride back up. About six thousand seven hundred square feet of it. Walls,
floor, and the sides of the escalator you are standing on. Letters up to twelve
feet tall.

So do this. At the top, before you step on, look down the shaft. That is the one
place you see the whole thing at once, and it is the only shot worth taking.

Red, white and black, nothing else. That is not styling, that is a career.
Kruger spent her twenties as an editorial designer at Conde Nast, on
Mademoiselle, and she was head designer inside a year, at twenty two. The job
was to stop a stranger walking past a newsstand, in about one second, using
type. Everything around you now is that same skill turned in a different
direction. The typeface is Futura Bold Oblique, drawn in 1924 by Paul Renner, a
face built out of circles and straight lines because it was meant to look like
the future. Slant it, set it huge, put it on red, and it reads as an
advertisement before you have understood a single word. She lets it. Then the
words are not selling you anything.

Now look at the floor, and notice that you are standing on it. This is the one
work in the museum you are supposed to walk on. Underfoot it says WHO IS BEYOND
THE LAW. WHO IS FREE TO CHOOSE. WHO SPEAKS. WHO IS SILENT. Four questions, no
question marks in the usual place, no answers offered.

Read those, then remember where you are standing. Ten minutes east along this
lawn is the Capitol. There is a floor in a national museum asking who is beyond
the law, and it has been asking it since 2012.

Somewhere else on the floor: YOU WANT IT. YOU BUY IT. YOU FORGET IT. The shop is
a few steps away. Make of that what you like.

The title is the part people get backwards. It is not belief versus doubt. It is
a plus sign. Both, at once, held together, which is a harder thing to do than
picking one and is the whole argument of the room. Kruger is not telling you
what to think here, which would be easy and would make her one more voice in a
hall of voices. She is asking whether you can stand to be uncertain for ten
minutes.

One practical thing. This was commissioned as a long term installation rather
than a permanent one, so it has been down there for years but it is vinyl on a
wall, not marble. If it matters to you, check before you come.

And one last habit worth breaking. People take a photograph of the wall. Take
one of the floor, with your own shoes in it. That is the piece.
