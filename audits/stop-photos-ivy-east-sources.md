## Reuse and display contract

- `src` is a remotely hosted 960px-wide Wikimedia thumbnail, except for the private-preview Sundial image hosted directly by Columbia. No image was downloaded. Some originals are portrait photographs; avoid an extreme crop that removes the destination.
- Show the supplied creator credit, linked `sourceUrl`, and license beside each image. Make `licenseUrl` clickable when provided. Preserve attribution and the license applicable to any adaptation; do not imply creator or university endorsement.
- CC BY and CC BY-SA versions are exactly the versions declared on each file page. Some pages offer multiple licenses; the JSON selects the named Creative Commons option. Public-domain entries rely on the explicit declaration on the linked file page. CC0 entries include the actual waiver URL.
- The 41 Wikimedia images use the cited open-license/public-domain declarations. The Sundial photograph has **no established open reuse license** and carries both `previewOnly: true` and `needsRightsReview: true`, with a `rightsNote`. Public endpoints and production must exclude it until rights are established or an exact reusable replacement is available. Private-preview inclusion is not permission to publish it. This is a source/permission inventory, not a guarantee about all possible non-copyright rights or a claim that a university endorses the site.
- Photographs remain unedited here. Display resizing uses Wikimedia's thumbnail service; any later substantive edit needs an appropriate changes notice.
- File-page metadata is sometimes incomplete: the College Walk 2024 page has malformed author markup, but the visible source text and own-work upload identify **Wil540 art**. The JSON uses that actual attribution. Do not interpret the empty machine-readable Artist field as anonymous.
- Harvard Square's photographer Wgreaves asks for a courtesy note when the photo is used outside Wikipedia. The file itself is CC BY-SA 4.0. No email or other unsolicited message was sent.

## Identity checks and rejected false matches

- Harvard Art Museums: selected 2018 photograph shows the modern museum exterior and glass roof. Rejected old standalone Sackler / old Fogg buildings and pictures of collection artworks.
- Yale Phelps Gate: rejected `Yale University - Outside Phelps Gate - New Haven - CT - USA (7093118031).jpg`; it shows an adjacent streetscape, not the gate. The selected Nick Allen photograph shows the actual gateway beneath Phelps Hall.
- Yale Old Campus: the selected photograph shows its path and Durfee Hall archway. Cross Campus is independently pictured as the tree-lined walk above Bass Library, not reused from Old Campus.
- Yale Art Gallery and Center for British Art: actual building views, not artworks from their collections. Woolsey Hall caption accurately includes the adjoining domed Memorial Rotunda visible in the frame.
- Princeton Prospect House: selected 2026 exterior is the Italianate house. The art museum is a separate, visually verified March 2026 photograph of the rebuilt museum. No photograph of the demolished former museum is used.
- Princeton paired stop: East Pyne has a courtyard/arched-passage exterior; Chancellor Green has a clearly labeled 1997 interior skylight detail. The detail is not presented as its facade or a contemporary image.
- Princeton Chapel is a labeled interior nave photograph, not an exterior. Alexander Hall is the university concert hall, not the similarly named Princeton Theological Seminary building.
- Blair Arch: replaced a 332-by-443-pixel candidate with a 1970-by-2731-pixel CC0 photograph of the clock tower and passage.
- Columbia College Walk: rejected `Columbia University Court Yard 01.jpg` after seeing that it shows a different campus courtyard despite its broad metadata. The selected 2024 image actually shows College Walk beside Low Plaza.
- Columbia Philosophy Hall: selected the actual carved entrance, not a bookplate or a statue-only photograph. The broader `Columbia University, NYC (June 2014) - 25.JPG` candidate was not used; it frames a different building as the primary subject.
- Columbia Pupin Hall: rejected `Pupin Hall Columbia.jpg`, which showed interior plaques rather than the building; the selected Ajay Suresh image shows the full facade and observatory dome.
- Columbia St Paul's Chapel: the Morningside Heights campus chapel, not lower Manhattan's St Paul's Chapel.

## The Sundial: private-preview gap closed; public-use rights remain unresolved

The current monument is a low, circular granite base on the south edge of College Walk. Columbia's [Event Management site](https://eventmanagement.columbia.edu/content/sundial) describes and pictures this exact surviving base and states that the cracked sphere was removed in 1946. The selected [official photograph](https://eventmanagement.columbia.edu/sites/eventmanagement.columbia.edu/files/content/Venues/Outdoor%20Spaces/Sundial.jpg) was opened both on that source page and directly in the browser on 2026-09-12: the base is prominent in the center foreground, its relief panels are visible, and there is no sphere. Butler Library provides background context rather than substituting for the Sundial. Its natural size is 1234 × 629 pixels. No image was downloaded or edited.

The university page carries a Columbia University copyright notice, but provides neither a named photographer nor an open reuse grant. The JSON credit therefore identifies the source institution without inventing a photographer; its date is explicitly undated. Per the bounded private-preview instruction, this source is recorded with `previewOnly: true`, `needsRightsReview: true`, and an explicit `rightsNote`. **Do not publish, merge into public photo responses, or count it as rights-cleared production coverage.** A permission-cleared site-owned photograph of the same base or a focused openly reusable replacement is still needed for production.

Rejected as current destination photographs:

- [Columbiasundial.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Columbiasundial.jpg): historical postcard showing the sphere that is no longer present.
- [ColumbiaSundialCeremony.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:ColumbiaSundialCeremony.jpg): historical dedication photograph; not today's appearance. Its caption date also conflicts with Columbia's 1914 account, so it is not used for dating.
- [Nathan Arrington's Columbia Sundial 82](https://www.flickr.com/photos/nathanarrington/12008011323/): current-era photograph, but all rights reserved.
- [Bob the Wonderer's students around the Sundial](https://www.flickr.com/photos/bobthewonderer/50560940363/): all rights reserved.

The separately selected Butler Library photograph does incidentally show a small Sundial base, but it is not counted as a focused Sundial photo or relabeled to manufacture coverage. The new university image independently and prominently depicts the base.

Separate narrative fact check for the owner, **not changed in this work**: the tour's 1885 installation wording confuses the donor class with the installation. [Columbia Magazine](https://magazine.columbia.edu/article/all-not-lost) identifies the sphere as erected in 1914, while [Columbia News](https://news.columbia.edu/news/6-iconic-inscriptions-columbias-morningside-campus-you-should-know) identifies the donor as the Class of 1885.
