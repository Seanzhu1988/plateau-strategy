# Architecture viewer stories: source and alignment ledger

Checked: 2026-09-12.

Scope: `architecture-stories.js` adds a supplemental written layer to the ten
stable architecture scene keys and a `peace-fountain` photograph story. It does not replace any narrated tour stop,
change audio order, synthesize speech, call a model API or incur API charges.
Each story has matching English and simplified Chinese paragraphs and three
short visual cues. Chinese uses 波士顿公园 for Boston Common, matching the
traveler-facing terminology requested for the tours.

## Evidence method

Historical claims were checked afresh against the responsible institutions:
National Park Service, the operating churches and museums, SOM as tower
architect, and the National September 11 Memorial & Museum. These include
institutional interpretations of archival evidence, not a claim that each
webpage is itself an eighteenth-century primary document. No historical date
or event is justified solely by the legacy geometry headers.

Model observations use the separately reviewed physical reconstructions and
their source audits. The prose distinguishes observation from interpretation:
invitations to look, pause, compare or reflect are our writing, not quotations
or purported historical testimony. No speech, witness quotation or invented
victim name is included. Sources are paraphrased rather than transcribed.
No third-party images are republished by these files. The existing licensed
Peace Fountain photograph was inspected for its three visual cues. At the
user's request, public paragraphs tell place history, craft and visible forms;
model limitations and review language are confined to this internal ledger.

## Per-scene claim ledger

### world-trade-center

- Tower completion in 2014, eight triangular faces, octagonal middle, rotated
  square roof and 1,776-foot height: [SOM project account](https://www.som.com/projects/one-world-trade-center/),
  project facts and the section describing the faceted form.
- Arad and Walker, pools within both former tower footprints, two-stage
  descent and central voids: [Memorial design account](https://www.911memorial.org/visit/memorial/about-memorial),
  Making the Memorial and Memorial Pools sections.
- Separate counts of 2,977 victims in 2001 and six in the 1993 bombing:
  [Memorial overview](https://www.911memorial.org/visit/memorial), opening text.
- Bronze names and relationships in their arrangement are supported by the
  Memorial design account's names section. Names are deliberately absent from
  the model. The place story does not instruct visitors to read or search an
  illustrated panel, and does not suggest that the tower replaces the pools.
- Omitted features remain omitted from the written visual cues: Survivor
  Tree, Museum pavilion, Memorial Glade and Oculus. No visitor hours, fees,
  access promises or unverified current site conditions are given.

### state-house

- Bulfinch, 1798 completion, Boston Common setting and the three petitioning
  histories: [NPS State House page](https://www.nps.gov/places/massachusetts-state-house.htm),
  main historical paragraphs. The same source supports the 1855 education
  desegregation statement. Charles Bulfinch is the standard spelling; the
  NPS page's doubled-letter rendering is not reproduced.
- Dome, brick arches, columned portico and pediment are observable features
  in the reviewed model. The story describes this frontage without claiming
  coverage of the later rear extensions or full current government complex.
- Closing reflection about representation is editorial interpretation of
  the documented petitions, not an attributed historical quotation.

### park-street

- 1809 founding, 217-foot 9-inch steeple, city-requested clock in 1820:
  [Church's own Freedom Trail account](https://www.parkstreet.org/about-us/freedom-trail/),
  Founding and Architecture sections.
- Garrison's 1829 antislavery speech:
  [NPS Park Street page](https://www.nps.gov/places/park-street-church.htm).
  The prose does not expand this into an unsupported claim of the first
  antislavery speech in America.
- Brick clock stage, open upper white stages, four entrance columns and
  pitched nave roof match the model's corrected exterior. No interior
  stained glass, bells or moving mechanism is promised as visible.

### old-south

  does not imply that tea was destroyed inside the building.

### old-state-house

- 1713 construction, colonial government and merchants' exchange, and July

  [NPS Old State House page](https://www.nps.gov/places/old-state-house.htm).

  west-gable eagle, which is not a focus of this reconstruction.

### faneuil-hall

- Market and meeting hall in 1742, Bulfinch enlargement in 1805 and 1806,
  Drowne's 1742 gilded copper grasshopper and benefactor's slave trafficking:
  [NPS virtual tour](https://home.nps.gov/bost/learn/faneuil-hall-virtual-tour.htm),
  introduction, Peter Faneuil, General Architectural Style of Exterior and
  Grasshopper Weathervane sections.
- Later abolitionist, suffrage and labor organizing:
  [NPS Faneuil Hall page](https://www.nps.gov/places/faneuil-hall.htm).
- The text distinguishes the expanded exterior from the smaller 1742 hall.
  It does not repeat the undocumented spy-test legend as fact. The closing
  discussion of liberty is editorial synthesis, not an invented quotation.
- Arched openings, barrel dormers, cupola and small three-dimensional
  grasshopper are present. Exact sculpture and original-brick inventory are
  not claimed by the story or model.

### paul-revere

- Approximate 1680 construction, family residence for much of 1770 to 1800,
  later commercial uses and 1908 restoration:
  [NPS Paul Revere House page](https://www.nps.gov/places/paul-revere-house.htm).
- Upper-floor projection, paired casements and skew rear wing:
  [HABS front elevation](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ma0478.sheet.00001a/)
  and [HABS measured plan](https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/ma0478.sheet.00002a/).
  These drawings were inspected in the separate architecture source review;
  the new historical narrative independently uses the NPS residence history.
- End chimney and broad front rooms:
  [House museum's street-view account](https://www.paulreverehouse.org/a-street-view-of-the-paul-revere-house/).
  Chandler, 1907 and 1908 restoration, physical evidence and restored earlier
  features: [Museum's architectural restoration account](https://www.paulreverehouse.org/reviving-the-revere-house-an-architectural-viewpoint-on-the-homes-restoration/).
- The house is presented as restored, without an unchanged-1775 interior
  claim. No bedroom inventory, family dialogue or invented domestic scene
  is narrated. The current model follows the corrected HABS plan, not the
  obsolete 48-by-30-foot legacy main block.

### old-north

- 1723 Christ Church and April 18, 1775 lantern event:
  [NPS Old North page](https://www.nps.gov/places/old-north-church.htm).
- Water route, Charlestown-side warning network, Revere's participation,
  storm losses and third spire installed in 1955:
  [Church's steeple history](https://oldnorth.com/steeple-bell-chamber/),
  lantern-signal and three-steeples sections.
- The story does not state that Revere himself displayed the lanterns or
  that he waited for them as the sole recipient. It does not present the
  current white timber spire as surviving unchanged from 1775.
- Paired lower bell-stage openings, upper lantern and pointed spire match
  the corrected model. The commemorative plaque's inscription is not
  promised as readable, nor are the two historic lanterns modeled as
  permanently lit objects.

### constitution

- Boston launch in 1797 and War of 1812 nickname:
  [NPS ship overview](https://www.nps.gov/places/uss-constitution.htm).
- Wooden hull rather than iron armor:
  [NPS account of Old Ironsides](https://www.nps.gov/articles/old-ironsides.htm).
  No sailor's supposedly verbatim exclamation is quoted.
- Later maintained quarter galleries:
  [Museum/NHHC finishing work](https://ussconstitutionmuseum.org/2017/07/07/finishing-touches/).
  Separate small upper stern windows:
  [NHHC historians' 2016 repair record](https://ussconstitutionmuseum.org/2016/03/03/stern-repairs/).
- The model's current white-and-black stern is not mislabeled as its exact
  original battle configuration. Bare yards, three masts, white gun-port band
  and windowed galleries are visible; no fully deployed sails or detailed
  below-deck rooms are promised. Chinese 老铁甲 is a nickname immediately
  clarified as not denoting an iron-armored hull.

### bunker-hill

- Breed's Hill location, June 17, 1775 battle and redoubt, British retention
  of the field with heavy losses, dedication in 1843:
  [NPS battle and monument account](https://www.nps.gov/bost/learn/historyculture/bhm.htm).
- Height 221 feet 5 inches, narrowing granite courses, observation chamber,
  294 granite stairs, initial horse-powered lifting and later steam engine,
  and pyramidal cap:
  [NPS monument anatomy](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/bhm-by-the-numbers.htm).
- The story avoids calling the battle a straightforward colonial victory.
  The internal staircase is a site-history fact, not a promised interior
  view. No climb availability, ticket or access guarantee is stated.

### peace-fountain

- Greg Wyatt, 1985 and the diocesan bicentennial:
  [Cathedral's own history](https://www.stjohndivine.org/about), 1985 entry.
- Bronze monument, Michael, defeated Satan, giraffe, sun and moon, and the
  dedication to children's creativity:
  [Artist's monument account and plaque transcription](https://gregwyattsculpture.com/bronze/peace-fountain).
  The caption's symbolic interpretation is attributed to the artwork; it is
  not presented as a universal religious assertion.
- Cast bronze as Wyatt's sculptural medium:
  [Artist's account of his practice](https://gregwyattsculpture.com/about).
  No unsupported foundry, mold material, casting temperature, lost-wax process,
  assembly method or exact original surface finish is asserted. Raised edges,
  overlapping feathers and textured drapery are visible in the existing
  licensed Jay Dobkin photograph.
- Schoolchildren modeled the surrounding animals, subsequently realized
  in bronze: [Cathedral's 2017 exhibition account](https://www.stjohndivine.org/about/blog/1/posts/25/a-summer-of-sculpture-animals-of-freedom)
  and the artist's monument account. The story does not transfer an individual
  child's work or a foundry credit to Wyatt without evidence.
- The earlier conversation called Michael's adversary a dragon. The artist's
  own identification is Satan. The story uses that documented identification
  rather than inventing an explicitly dragon-shaped figure. It avoids graphic
  decapitation details and does not promise working water jets.
- This supplemental key is a photograph story, not an eleventh 3D model.
  Its visual cues refer only to the wing, giraffe and bronze texture actually
  shown in the current image. No rotation or hidden-photo-detail prompt is used.

## Integration and verification

- Export: `ARCHITECTURE_STORIES`; the ten stable model keys plus the requested
  supplemental `peace-fountain` photo key.
- Each entry: `en`, `zh`, `sources`. Each locale: `title`, two or three
  `paragraphs`, three short `lookFor` strings.
- English target is 100 to 150 words per scene, with up to 180 for the
  combined tower/memorial story. Paragraph word counts are checked without
  counting titles, cue labels or citations.
- No em dash or en dash occurs in reader-facing text. There is no HTML,
  inline script, remote execution, API fetch, generated audio or new media.
- These stories add an interpretive layer. Their presence does not alter the
  model audit, make an approximate dimension surveyed, or certify current
  real-world opening conditions. Independent final content/render checks
  belong to the parent release task.
- Actual English paragraph counts: WTC 161; State House 109; Park Street 112;
  Old South 123; Old State House 114; Faneuil 119; Paul Revere 131; Old North
  117; Constitution 125; Bunker Hill 115; Peace Fountain 130.
- Node import and assertions passed: eleven exact keys, paired locale
  structure, two/three paragraphs, three visual cues, HTTPS source URLs,
  paragraph word budgets, no long dashes, and no public model/QA vocabulary.
