The Metropolitan Museum,
an indoor guide blueprint

Sixteen corridors mapped across two floors, the place where a map goes grey and footprints take over.

The Met is a mile of galleries where your phone stops knowing where it is. This sheet lays the guide across it as a small network of corridors. Each corridor is a short walked path between two named rooms. Follow the footprints and the guide names each room as you reach it. Turn the wrong way and the footprints vanish until you face the way through again.

The ground floor

The Great Hall is the hub, the room you walk into. From it the guide reaches left into Egyptian Art and on to the Temple of Dendur and the American Wing court, up into Arms and Armor and across to Medieval Art and the Lehman Wing, and down to Greek and Roman, Modern Art, and the Grand Staircase that carries you to the upper floor.

The upper floor

The Grand Staircase arrives beside European Paintings. From there the guide runs out to the nineteenth century galleries, across to Asian Art and the Astor Court, and around through Islamic Art back to the stair. A loop you can walk without doubling back.

How one corridor guides you

A corridor is not an arrow. It is the footprints of the person who walked it before you, laid on the floor ahead. When you face the way through, they light the path and the guide names what is beside you. When you turn around, they disappear, because a footprint pointing back the way you came is not a direction, it is a distraction. That is the whole of the gate.

What is mapped, and what awaits a walk

The sixteen corridors are named and placed, but not one of them has been walked yet. A corridor holds no footprints until a person records it once, phone in hand, from the first room to the second. Until that walk this is a plan of where the guide will live, not yet a guide. The first recorded corridor turns one line on this sheet into something a stranger can follow.

Why the Met

It is the hardest easy case. Indoors, so GPS is useless, which is exactly where footprints earn their keep. Enormous, so people genuinely get lost. And loved, so the moment someone reaches the room they came for is worth protecting. If the guide carries a stranger here, it carries one in an airport, a hospital, a station.

This sheet says what the thing is and where it goes. It does not publish how a corridor is recorded, or how the gate decides you have turned around. Those are the working parts, and they stay off the page.
