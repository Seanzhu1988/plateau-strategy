Robotic trading, the concept

Shared with you by link. Not on the website, not finished, and not asking you for anything.

This page takes nothing from you. No money, no bank account, no card, no signup. There is no form on this page that could start any of those, because none has been built.

It is a written-up idea, sent to a few people for their honest reaction. It is not an offer to sell anything, not an invitation to invest, and not financial advice.

The short version: software that places trades on its own, according to rules written in advance, and reports what it did. Not a prediction engine and not a machine that knows where the market is going, a machine that follows instructions without getting bored, scared or hopeful.

What a trading robot actually is

A person trading by hand does four things: watches prices, decides, places the order, and records the result. A trading robot does the same four things, except the deciding step is a rule someone wrote down beforehand, "if this, then that", and it never deviates from it.

That is the whole trick, and it is smaller than it sounds. The robot has no opinion. Its only real advantages over a person are that it never sleeps, never talks itself out of its own plan, and can be tested against years of past prices in an afternoon.

Its disadvantages are the same size. A rule that worked in one kind of market keeps being followed when the market changes. It cannot tell the difference between a strategy that stopped working and a run of bad luck. And a rule that was quietly fitted to past data will look brilliant in a test and do nothing in real life, this is the single most common way these projects fail, and it is very hard to see from the inside.

The idea

Most people carrying expensive debt cannot get out of it by trying harder. The interest compounds faster than the willpower lasts. The idea is to attack that with something that does not need willpower: a set of trading rules, run automatically, with anything it earns pointed at the debt rather than back into the market.

The part that makes it different from the usual pitch is the part that makes it slow: the results have to be real and provable before anyone is asked for a cent. That means a track record built with our own money, timestamped, including the losing months, before there is a product at all.

Why nobody's money is being touched

Partly because it would be wrong to take money for something unproven. But also because of what handling other people's money legally is.

The moment you pool other people's funds and trade them, or take a share of what you make for them, you are in territory the SEC and the state of Washington regulate closely, adviser registration, custody rules, disclosure obligations, and personal liability that does not go away because everyone involved is a friend. Those rules exist because this exact idea, minus the honesty, is one of the oldest ways people get robbed.

So the sequence is: prove it with our own money first, get the legal structure right second, and only then talk to anyone about theirs. This page is step zero, showing you the thinking, at the stage where the only thing at stake is my own time.

What could go wrong, plainly

You are reading this to react to it, so here is what I would push on if someone showed it to me:

  • A strategy can be right and still lose. Any position that is down is a real loss while it is down. Holding it instead of selling does not undo that, it only postpones writing it in the ledger. Any version of this that describes not-selling as protection is describing a feeling, not a safeguard.
  • Backtests lie by default. Rules tuned until they look good on past prices will look good on past prices. The only test that counts is money at risk, forward in time, with the dates recorded before the result is known.
  • Crypto moves far more than most people plan for. Drawdowns of half the account have happened repeatedly, to competent people, in living memory.
  • Big monthly percentages are the reddest flag in finance. The best-known funds in the world are proud of numbers that sound unimpressive next to what gets promised in this corner of the internet. If a version of this ever quotes you a comfortable monthly return, treat that as the thing to interrogate first, and that goes for a version of it with my name on it.
  • The regulation is not a formality. Getting the structure wrong is not a fine, it is the end of the business.
